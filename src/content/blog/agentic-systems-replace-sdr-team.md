Every growing company hits the same wall. Revenue targets demand more pipeline, pipeline demands more SDRs, SDRs demand management — and suddenly your burn multiple is moving the wrong way while your best rep spends the morning copying data between tabs.

The uncomfortable truth is that most sales development work isn't selling. It's **repetition**: researching accounts, writing the first touch, chasing replies, keeping the CRM honest. That work has to happen, but it almost never needs a human to happen well.

## What actually gets automated

When we map a go-to-market motion, we separate the parts that need judgement from the parts that just need to be done reliably. The second bucket is where agents live:

- **Research** — an agent builds a dossier per target account: tech stack, hiring signals, funding, and the specific trigger that makes them buyable *now*.
- **Outbound** — sequences grounded in that dossier, where every message cites a real, verifiable observation rather than a spun template.
- **Inbound qualification** — every lead answered in seconds, scored against your ICP, and routed to the right owner with context attached.
- **CRM hygiene** — deduping, enriching, and closing stale opportunities so your forecast means something.

None of this is a chatbot answering questions. It's a system doing the work.

## What stays human

The goal was never to remove people — it's to point them at the part of the job that compounds. A founder or AE walking into a call should already know the account's context, the likely objection, and the comparable customer to reference. The relationship, the negotiation, the judgement call on a non-standard deal — that stays with a person.

A good rule of thumb: **if the task has a right answer, an agent should do it. If it needs taste, a human should.**

## The economics

The reason this matters isn't novelty — it's the shape of the cost curve. A traditional SDR function scales linearly: more pipeline means more hires means more management. An agent fleet scales with your ad spend and your data, not your payroll. You review a handful of escalations a week instead of running a team.

That's the whole thesis behind agentic systems: **engineer the leverage, so the company scales in revenue, not headcount.**

If your team is spending its day on work that repeats, there's usually a system underneath it waiting to be built.
